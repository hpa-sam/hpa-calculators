Assumptions and limits
  • Weight distribution needs only the four corner weights and is independent of the lift.
  • The height formula is exact for a rigid car and uses the level wheelbase. No lift-angle correction is needed, as those terms cancel.
  • It assumes the suspension does not move between the level and raised readings. Settle the car, and restrain or solid-mount the suspension for best results.
  • Tyre radius is treated as constant. It changes slightly with load and pressure, and that error carries into the height.
  • Lift angle dominates accuracy. Below about 10 degrees the result is not worth much.
  • The car is assumed symmetrical enough that the average track represents where the centre of gravity sits laterally.
  • Figures are for the exact ride height, fuel load and ballast on the scales at the time.
