Assumptions and limits
  • Displacement is swept volume only.
  • Compression ratios are only as good as the chamber, deck, gasket and dish figures entered. Deck clearance is the most sensitive.
  • Gasket volume assumes a round opening of the stated bore and compressed thickness.
  • Dish or dome is a single figure, so valve reliefs must be included in it.
  • Dynamic compression uses the piston position at intake closing. It ignores gas dynamics and cam ramp, so treat it as comparative, not as cylinder pressure.
  • Reciprocating loads ignore rod mass, gas pressure and friction. Peak rod tension is the inertia load at TDC on overlap, which is the case that sizes rod bolts.
  • Piston motion assumes a conventional crank with no pin or crank offset.
  • All figures are estimates for planning. Measure the parts you actually have.
